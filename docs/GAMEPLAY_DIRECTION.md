# Gameplay direction — memorable, fair, replayable

This is a design proposal, not a claim that all features are implemented.

## Core 8–12 minute loop
1. **Choose a clear micro-goal** from a quest, landmark, NPC clue or collection milestone.
2. **Explore a readable route** with a visual landmark, optional shortcut and one secret worth investigating.
3. **Encounter with agency**: grass rustle/region sign tells the player what kind of risk/reward is nearby; allow a short escape or safe retreat.
4. **Make a tactical decision**: choose a move/status/switch or spend a limited item; telegraph boss/trainer strategy rather than surprise one-shot.
5. **Get a meaningful result**: new species data, an item with immediate utility, a move/evolution, a story reveal or a map shortcut.
6. **Return, heal, organize and plan**. The player should feel ready for the next expedition, not trapped in a grind.

## Make collection satisfying
- Each species needs a distinct silhouette, palette, idle behavior and one recognizable field/battle reaction.
- Use a discovery card on first encounter/catch with rarity, habitat and one short original lore line.
- Avoid making rarity the only motivation: add habitat clues, research sets and NPC requests that reward exploring different zones.
- Make capture odds legible enough to learn: low HP/status/ball choice should visibly change the odds; show feedback after shakes.
- Keep duplicate catches useful but not mandatory (research tokens, optional release/reward, breeding only if it adds depth).

## Make battles strategic without being exhausting
- Teach type advantage in the first safe battle; show clear effectiveness cues.
- Give each early trainer a readable theme and counter-play, not just higher levels.
- Limit repetitive wild battles: quick resolution for trivial encounters, optional repel/avoidance tools, or a player-controlled encounter toggle after tutorial.
- Bosses need a signature mechanic, a warning phase and a reward that unlocks a new option/route.
- Do not punish experimentation with expensive consumables or long walkbacks.

## Quest and progression architecture
- Main quest: 3–5 beats per area, each ends with a new access, tool, teammate or narrative question.
- Side quests: 1) collection, 2) navigation/secret, 3) trainer challenge, 4) creature behavior; reward different item types.
- Milestone reward should be one-time, visible in quest journal and stored in save flags; never silently grant duplicate farming.
- Region progression: starter → route skill/tutorial → first rival/trainer → habitat discovery → local boss → route unlock → new region hook.
- Each new map should ship with encounter table, 3–5 NPCs with authored dialog, 1 landmark, 2 secrets, 1 story objective, 2 side activities and a safe/heal point.

## UX polish players notice
- One objective at a time, optional quest journal, minimap/compass or directional landmark.
- Consistent button prompts (Z/Enter, Escape, touch); dialogue fast-forward and replayable tutorial.
- Battle log speed setting, animation skip, text speed, mute/music sliders and reduced-motion option.
- Explicit save confirmation/export and visible autosave indicator.
- End the first area with a memorable choice/reveal, not merely a counter reaching 100%.

## Playtest questions
Record at least 5 new players (or a larger online test) without coaching:
- Can they find the starter and first catch within 5 minutes?
- Can they explain why a catch failed and what to try next?
- Do they discover one optional secret in 15 minutes?
- Can they finish the first route without a soft lock or resource starvation?
- Which battle/quest did they skip, and why?
- At what minute did they stop feeling curious?
